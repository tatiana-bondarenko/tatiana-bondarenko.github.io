In my work I argue that *triviality* and [L(ogical)-analyticity](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/On-Analyticity-in-Natural-Language-Gajewski/966692f722bd9153bc1f531b174369d181d15a05) are responsible for many, if not all, selectional restrictions of clause-embedding predicates: certain combinations of predicates and clauses might be syntactically well-formed, but lead to trivial meanings, and such semantic deviance leads to ungrammaticality. The idea that semantic triviality can lead to ungrammaticality can help us explain [why clauses that are verbal modifiers tend to be bigger in size](https://ling.auf.net/lingbuzz/007327), [why polarity-sensitive subjunctives appear only with some clause-embedding verbs](https://ling.auf.net/lingbuzz/007471), and [why stative 'think' cannot embed questions](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1zkIsnLyTOwteUhoB4pmAtJkcg1DVYXCj/view). I also proposed that [true conjunction of full-sized embedded clauses (CPs) is impossible in natural languages](https://ling.auf.net/lingbuzz/007884) due to the meanings of elements in the clausal left peripheries: such conjunctions are always either trivially false or redundant, leading to their ungrammaticality. This proposal allows us to explain [a cross-linguistic generalization](https://journals.linguisticsociety.org/proceedings/index.php/SALT/article/view/30.583): whenever 'CP and CP' strings are possible, the conjunction is compatible with Conjunction Reduction and has wide scope with respect to the embedding predicate.
