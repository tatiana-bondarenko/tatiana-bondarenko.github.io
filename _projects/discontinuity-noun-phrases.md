Languages vary in whether they allow discontinuous noun phrases: while in languages like English sentences with ''extraction of the left branch'' like *\*Every I saw lamp hanging from the ceiling* are ungrammatical, equivalent strings in languages like Russian are fully acceptable. I am interested in how such discontinuity arises, how it is restricted, and why languages vary in whether they allow discontinuous phrases. One hypothesis I argued for is [that this discontuity is only apparent](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/synt.12245): all languages move  only full noun phrases in syntax, and what they differ in is rules of copy pronounciation at PF---while Russian allows *distributed deletion* of noun phrases, English does not. Another interesting question is how linearization constraints restrict pronounciation of discontinuous noun phrases and whether they can explain [asymmetries in left branch extraction from subjects and objects](https://ojs.ung.si/index.php/JSL/article/view/130).
